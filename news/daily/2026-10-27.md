### 1) arXiv (structural frontier): "Monetizing Intelligence" — the ad-design math for LLM platforms has a published form now
- Track: ai_change
- Marker: 💡 idea
- Why it matters to me: This week's one structural AI item, and it's directly on AQ2's agent-commerce lens. The model: an LLM platform that runs ads jointly chooses **response quality, ad intensity, and subscription price** — and the finding is that when ad-supported and ad-free plans coexist, **ad revenue can finance useful service for consumers** (the ad subsidy makes the low/zero tier actually work, rather than being a cripple tier). That's the exact tradeoff shape for anything we build where AI does the work and someone monetizes attention vs. outcome.
- Tradeoff / tension: It's equilibrium theory, not field data — the "ad revenue funds useful service" claim is conditional on the quality-cost curve the paper assumes. If we borrow the shape for our own pricing (ad-light tier vs. outcome-priced tier), we inherit the assumption that ad-funded quality is stable; if ad revenue sags, the subsidized tier degrades and the whole structure wobbles. Choosing ad-supported pricing also means our product value becomes partly a function of a market (ad CPMs) we don't control.
- Implication: the useful output is a **design checklist for monetization architecture**, not a number: (1) what does the ad tier actually subsidize — response quality or convenience? (2) at what point does ad load destroy the trust layer we're selling on? (3) does the ad-free tier need to be genuinely good, or is it just a lead-gen hook? That checklist is what we'd run before pricing an agent-commerce or creator-matching product.

- Confidence: medium (frontier paper; the mechanism is sound, the parameter values are assumed)
