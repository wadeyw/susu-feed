### 2) So you want to use OpenRouter? — router abstraction causes behavioral drift
- Track: own_business
- Why it matters to me: Operator-level lesson on LLM infrastructure tradeoffs: aggregators that auto-fallback and auto-pick the cheapest backend make the *same* API endpoint behave differently depending on which provider serves it — different serving software, different optimizations, missing vision capability, different reasoning-effort handling. If any product I build routes through a model aggregator, "same model, same prompt" is not guaranteed same output.
- Tradeoff / tension: Concurrency/cost simplicity of a single routing endpoint vs. behavioral determinism. The escape hatches exist (`provider.only`, `/endpoints` to enumerate backends) but you must know they exist before you hit a subtle quality regression in production.
- Implication: When adopting LLM infrastructure, pin more than the model name — pin the serving path or run a golden-behavior test suite per provider. "Model X" is becoming a distribution family, not a deterministic service. This also hints at a product wedge: provider-drift monitoring as a boring-to-sell, real need.
- Action: watch — if I touch OpenRouter or any router for a project, add a small set of golden prompts with expected behavior per provider before trusting the default routing.
- Confidence: medium
- Source: Simon Willison — https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/11/so-you-want-to-use-openrouter/

### 3) OpenAI: scaling a custom storage platform (Habitat) for 1B+ ChatGPT users
- Track: ai_change
- Why it matters to me: Institutional signal, not a product launch: OpenAI rebuilt storage from an internal Python library into a globally distributed platform doing 22M requests/second. The competitive bottleneck for frontier AI companies is increasingly *infrastructure economics* (storage, networking, power) rather than model capability — the thing that compounds and that you can't buy off the shelf at scale.
- Tradeoff / tension: Vertical integration (build your own platform) buys control and cost at 1B-user scale, at the price of massive capex and distraction from the model layer. Smaller builders get the inverse: rent the infrastructure, but cede cost curves and latency to whoever owns the rails.
- Implication: When evaluating AI business timing, the question is no longer "can the model do X?" but "who owns the cost structure under X?" Products whose margins depend on inference/storage costs are being squeezed from below; products that sit on top of the rails have a stable-ish margin until the next price war.
- Action: think — for any idea on the business shortlist, check whether its gross margin depends on LLM/inference pricing. If yes, it's exposed to an arms race it can't win.
- Confidence: medium
- Source: OpenAI News — https://openai.com/index/scaling-storage-one-billion-users-part-one
