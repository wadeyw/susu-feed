### 2) Self-monitoring fails in situ: LLMs steer on injected uncertainty tokens, but can't reliably feel their own errors
- Track: ai_change
- Marker: 💡
- Why it matters to me: AQ2, and a direct follow-up to yesterday's Claude Ship gate. The popular pattern is asking agents to verbalize confidence to trigger backtracking. New four-open-weights-model result (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7x): *steering* works — an injected uncertainty token causally changes the trajectory — but *in-situ monitoring fidelity* is poor: confidence doesn't reliably drop when an error actually occurs. So an agent-drafted post or match that self-reports "I'm confident" is not a trustworthy gate input.
- Tradeoff / tension: Self-reported confidence is cheap and feels right, but it's untrustable exactly at the moment it matters (the error). Ex-post external checks (human override rate, sampled spot-audits, known-answer probes) cost more but are honest. Speed/cheapness vs. reliability — same tension as 11-24's routine/exception split, now with a reason not to let the agent draw the line itself.
- Implication: For our agent-approval gate: exclude self-reported confidence as a gate signal; use uncertainty *injection* for retries, and track two external numbers — human override rate + sampled external validation pass rate. Override rate staying high means the routine line is wrong, not that the agent is bad.
- Action: think — 15-minute note: which current or planned gates rely on the model's self-report? List them, replace with external signals.
- Confidence: medium-high (arXiv preprint, four open-weights models; check results don't obviously extend to frontier closed models)
- Source: arXiv cs.AI — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31228v1
