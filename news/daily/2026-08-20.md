### 1) Extensible Software in the Age of LLMs
- Track: ai_change / own_business
- Why it matters to me: This updates the mental model of what a "product" is. The architecture is shifting from monolithic application → accountable core + AI-authored extensions. If you're building anything that users will customize or extend, the cost structure has fundamentally changed: LLMs lower authoring cost, sandbox primitives lower deployment cost. This is a wedge strategy — build the trusted core, let AI fill the edges.
- Tradeoff / tension: Extensibility vs. accountability. The more you let users (via LLMs) extend your product, the less you control the experience. Sandboxing reduces risk but adds complexity. The core must be strong enough to anchor the ecosystem, or it collapses into incoherence.
- Implication: For any business I build, the architecture decision is no longer just "what features to ship" — it's "what should be closed core vs. open extension surface." Products that design for AI-authored plugins from day one will have a cost advantage in coverage and a distribution advantage in community.

For a business, the question isn't "should we adopt crypto" — it's "which markets and which worker segments make the math work, and what's the regulatory exposure?"
- Implication: If I'm building a business that involves any global or remote work, the payout rail is a competitive advantage decision. Platforms that solve this well today will have lower friction and higher worker retention tomorrow. The Stripe data gives a concrete map of where demand is highest.
