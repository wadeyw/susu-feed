### 2) AI worms through Word: a new class of supply-chain attack
- Track: ai_change / better_decisions
- Why it matters to me: This isn't theoretical—this is a working vulnerability in a product millions use daily. As AI moves from chatbots into document manipulation, the threat model changes fundamentally. Cross-Domain Prompt Injection Attacks (XPIAs) enable self-propagating malware that spreads through normal workflows without the attacker needing any subsequent presence. This is a concrete example of how AI structurally alters security economics: one exploited trust boundary becomes an infinitely replicable vector.
- Tradeoff / tension: Productivity gains from AI-assisted authorship versus emergent attack surfaces that traditional security doesn't address. The architectural assumption that "documents are trusted input" breaks completely when documents carry executable instructions invisible to humans but visible to LMs. Mitigation requires either crippling AI features or redesigning trust boundaries—a neither-north choice currently unsolved.
- Implication: Enterprise AI adoption cannot proceed with conventional security assumptions. Anyone building or evaluating AI-integrated products must account for prompt injection not as an edge case but as a first-class threat class affecting document integrity, data exfiltration, and lateral movement within organizations. Also personal risk: opening untrusted documents in Copilot-mode is potentially dangerous.
- Action: Implement strict policies: treat externally sourced documents as untrusted when used with AI assistants, disable auto-processing of attachments, maintain human review checkpoints before trusting AI-edited output for distribution. Build this into both business processes and household practices.
- Confidence: medium-high (exploit demonstrated, class unmitigated, but real-world deployment prevalence unknown)

## Life / family nudge

When your daughters ask a question whose answer an AI could give in seconds, resist answering immediately. Instead, guide them through formulating the question precisely, identifying what they already know, sketching possible approaches, and evaluating which path yields the deepest understanding. The value isn't the answer—it's the workout of framing, hypothesizing, and checking. Do this consistently and you're building the cognitive immune system that will let them navigate an AI-saturated world without losing agency.
