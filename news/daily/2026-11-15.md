### 2) AgentSSL: MLE agents on unlabeled data — "labeled is scarce, unlabeled is abundant" is exactly our attribution regime
- Track: ai_change
- Marker: 💡 idea
- Why it matters to me: The paper (AgentSSL) reframes semi-supervised learning as a *program search problem*: agents automate pipeline design where labeled data is scarce but unlabeled data is abundant. That's our exact data regime — clean outcome labels (sales attributable to a creator post) are rare and expensive; engagement/signal data is plentiful. It's the strongest frontier pointer yet that attribution can be built *before* labels exist, then sharpened by labels — instead of waiting for clean tracking to have ROI.
- Tradeoff / tension: Semi-supervised pipelines borrow structure from unlabeled data, so systematic bias in engagement signals (e.g., paid boosts, follower inflation) silently poisons the labels that later get attached. The method improves cold-start; it does not remove the need for a handful of clean controlled campaigns as ground truth.
- Implication: Park as a methodology: cold-start matching/attribution from engagement patterns, with a small number of controlled holdout campaigns supplying the labels. It also reframes the client pitch — "we can start measuring on day one; labels compound the estimate" — which beats "wait until tracking is in place."
- Action: think — one note in the knowledge base: "attribution cold-start via semi-supervised search; labels as ground-truth anchors, not prerequisites." No build this week.
- Confidence: medium (solid paper framing; application to creator attribution is inference)
- Source: arXiv cs.AI — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17323v1
