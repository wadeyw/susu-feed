### 4) People don't want AI agents to contact their coworkers
- Track: better_decisions
- Why it matters to me: Greg Brockman observed that people strongly dislike when a coworker's ChatGPT contacts them asking for help — even though they'd happily do that same work if asked directly by a human. This reveals a fundamental tension in how AI agents will be adopted in social and professional contexts.
- Tradeoff / tension: AI agents can give people time back by handling coordination and requests autonomously, but they risk becoming a layer of unwanted intermediation that erodes trust and relationship quality. The tradeoff is efficiency of coordination vs. preservation of human connection.
- Implication: Any AI agent product that operates on behalf of users in social contexts (messaging, scheduling, collaboration) will face a adoption ceiling determined by how much people value the relationship itself. The winning design probably keeps humans in the loop for outreach, or makes the AI's involvement transparent. This is a design principle, not just a preference quirk.
- Action: think (when designing any agent that interacts with other humans, ask: would the recipient prefer this came from a person? If yes, how do you preserve that signal?)
