### 1) "Did a human read the code?" is the wrong proxy — review moved from a PR gate to a continuous check
- Track: ai_change
- Marker: 📌 watch
- Why it matters to me: Willison quotes a three-way synthesis (Addy Osmani, Morgan Westlee Lunt, Qing Wang) making one claim that reframes our workflow: the right question is **"what could go wrong with this change, and which check — automated or human — is the right one to catch it?"** In agent-heavy teams, review now runs continuously, "more like spellcheck while you write" — most changes have been reviewed by Claude at least once before a human looks. That's the same "bottleneck moved off generation" thread we logged on 10-15, but now applied to *quality control*: the scarce resource is not writing code, it's knowing *which* check catches *which* failure.
- Tradeoff / tension: Continuous AI pre-review reduces latency and catches mechanical errors, but it creates a false-confidence risk: if "Claude already looked at it" becomes the proxy for quality, human review retreats to rubber-stamping — and the failures that survive automated checks are exactly the judgment ones (wrong intent, stale business assumptions) that only a human catches. The cost of getting the split wrong is silent quality drift in the matching/copy pipeline, not a crash.
- Implication: The transferable move is building a **failure-mode catalog per pipeline stage**: for each stage (creator discovery, content gen, attribution), list what can go wrong and decide per item which check (automated, human, or both) is the right owner. That turns "review" from a vague habit into a dated, auditable allocation.
- Action: think — take one stage (say, creator matching) and draft a 5–7 line failure-mode catalog; mark each row automated / human / both. One hour, no code.
- Confidence: medium (Willison quoting three senior practitioners, 2026-10-16; the mechanism is well-argued, our transfer is inference)
- Source: Simon Willison — https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/16/code-review/
