- Track: ai_change
- Why it matters to me: AI coding agents are moving from novelty to daily tool. The bottleneck isn't generating code; it's knowing whether the generated code is correct without reading every line. - Implication: If you're building software with AI agents, invest in verification infrastructure (tests, contracts, staging validation) before you invest in agent capability. The agent is only as good as your ability to confirm its output. This shifts the bottleneck from AI quality to your own verification design.

### 2) Enterprise AI adoption now has a privacy infrastructure race
- Track: own_business
- Why it matters to me: OpenAI's Zero Data Retention for frontier models signals that data governance is becoming a product differentiator, not just a compliance checkbox. If you're evaluating AI platforms for anything that touches business data, this is now a decision criterion.
- Tradeoff / tension: ZDR keeps your data off OpenAI's training pipelines and abuse logs, but it may limit the safety monitoring that comes with data retention. The tradeoff is privacy versus the safety net of retained data for abuse detection. For enterprise customers, the calculation is clear: if your data is sensitive, ZDR is worth the reduced safety net. For smaller users, it may not matter.
- Implication: Trust infrastructure is becoming a competitive moat. Companies that can guarantee data control will win enterprise deals that others can't. For your own business, this means either building on platforms with strong ZDR guarantees or planning for on-prem/private deployment if your data is sensitive.
