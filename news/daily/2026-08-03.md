### 3) California's data broker enforcement just became real
- Track: own_business
- Why it matters to me: DROP (Delete Request Opt-out Platform) went live Aug 1. 350,000 Californians already signed up. Data brokers have 45 days to honor requests or face $200/day fines per affected resident. Twelve brokers have already been fined for failing to register. This is the first time a US state has built a centralized enforcement mechanism for data deletion — not just a right on paper, but a tool with teeth. Relevant to AQ3 (trust as a business constraint) and the watch topic on trust systems and payments infrastructure.
- Tradeoff / tension: Consumers gain a practical tool; data brokers face real enforcement cost. But the $200/day fine structure creates a incentive problem: brokers will calculate whether paying fines is cheaper than compliance, especially for non-California residents whose data they may still sell. The enforcement team is dedicated, but capacity is finite.
- Implication: Other states will copy this model. If you build any product that touches personal data, assume a federal DROP-like system is 2-4 years away. Design for the day your user can delete their data with one click — that day is coming.
- Action: Act. If you collect personal data, audit your deletion workflows now. If you build tools for creators or consumers, consider "data hygiene" as a feature differentiator.
