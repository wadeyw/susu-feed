### 2) What Link data tells us about AI spending
- Track: ai_change
- Why it matters to me: Stripe analyzed spending across 250M Link customers and found a real demand-side signal: the top 10% of AI spenders doubled their monthly spend from $183 to $359 in just 3 months (Dec 2025 → Mar 2026), after taking 22 months to go from $84 to $183. That acceleration is structural, not incremental. It suggests we're past the "experimental adoption" phase for a segment of users and into a "committed spend" phase. For anyone building in the AI tooling or agent space, this is a demand signal worth tracking.
- Tradeoff / tension: Spending more doesn't equal building sustainable businesses. These are consumers, not enterprises. The 50th percentile only went from $60 to $72 — most people are still dabbling. The tail is dramatic; the middle is flat.
- Implication: If you're building an AI product, the question isn't "do people want AI?" — the data says yes, at least for a segment. The question is whether you're targeting the accelerating tail or the stagnant middle, and what infrastructure (payments, agent wallets, spending controls) enables the tail to keep spending.
- Action: watch
- Confidence: medium
- Source: Stripe Blog — https://stripe.com/blog/what-link-data-tells-us-about-ai-spending

### 4) sqlite-utils 4.2: transform() with constraint preservation
- Track: remembering
- Why it matters to me: If you use sqlite-utils for knowledge management or local data workflows, this release improves the `table.transform()` operation to preserve check constraints, unique constraints, and column comments — things that previously got dropped during schema migrations. For anyone treating SQLite as an external memory system, losing constraints during transforms is a silent data-integrity risk.
- Tradeoff / tension: Preserving constraints makes transforms safer but slower (create- copy - drop - replace). The tradeoff is correctness vs. speed, and for knowledge systems where correctness matters more, this is a net win.
- Implication: If you're building or maintaining a local knowledge base with sqlite-utils, this reduces the risk of schema drift during migrations. Worth upgrading before doing any significant table restructuring.
