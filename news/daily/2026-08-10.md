### 3) SQLite compressed text-history prototypes
- Track: remembering
- Why it matters to me: Directly relevant to AQ5 — building external memory that compounds. Storing revision histories is a core problem for any knowledge system, and the zlib/zstd-on-JSON approach is a practical insight: compression makes historical storage essentially free when most content is repeated text.
- Tradeoff / tension: Full-text JSON arrays compress extremely well (repeated strings = high ratio) but sacrifice query efficiency. Normalized tables are query-friendly but add schema complexity. For a personal knowledge system where retrieval patterns are unpredictable, compression-first may win.
- Implication: Your knowledge base doesn't need to be a graph database to preserve context. A well-compressed JSON blob in SQLite may be the right answer for most personal systems — especially when the cost of complexity outweighs the benefit of querying individual revisions.
