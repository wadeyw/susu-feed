### 4) The Claude Opus 5 system prompt technique
- Track: better_decisions, remembering
- Why it matters to me: Anthropic handles post-training events by embedding a notice directly in the system prompt — not by retraining, not by hope. The Claude Opus 5 prompt includes a factual notice about the Fable/Mythos export control suspension, instructing the model to confirm events accurately, treat them as political topics, and check for newer information. This is a reusable technique: system prompts as a live-updatable knowledge layer.
- Tradeoff / tension: System prompt injection keeps models current without retraining cost, but it's fragile — prompts can be lost, overwritten, or inconsistent across model versions. It also raises a transparency question: how much of a model's "knowledge" is hardcoded instruction vs. learned representation? The technique works, but it's a bandage on a deeper problem (models have fixed training cutoffs).
- Implication: For your own knowledge system, treat the system prompt as a living document, not a one-time setup. The principle generalizes: external memory that can be updated without re-executing the full training process is a durable advantage. This is exactly what the SuSu Feed knowledge base is doing at scale.
