### 3) EdSurge "Missing Middle": the comprehension wall, and the "answers" vs "points back to text" AI distinction
- Track: family_future
- Marker: 📌 watch
- Why it matters to me: Two concrete findings from NWEA data (20M+ students) plus a practitioner (Coursemojo): (a) the lowest 10th percentile of middle-school readers is falling further behind every year even as scores recover elsewhere — early-grade literacy reforms stop working after grade 3; (b) the "comprehension wall" = text gets harder × background knowledge gets specialized: two kids with identical reading skill can perform two grade levels apart depending on what they already know. Key tool distinction: "indulgebots" (AI hands the answer) vs instructional AI (AI sends the reader back to the specific sentence to work it out).
- Tradeoff / tension: Real catch-up takes 2–3 years of above-average growth — not a one-off intervention. And background-knowledge advantage compounds (rich-get-richer on comprehension) unless deliberately built.
- Implication: Two rules for the daughters' roadmap: build topic knowledge early and deliberately (shared reading/watching topics); when choosing learning tools in a few years, reject tools that hand answers, pick tools that scaffold the struggle.
- Action: Watch — save the two-rule test in the family knowledge base; revisit at 2028 school-entry planning.
- Confidence: high (named researcher + practitioner, large dataset)
- Source: EdSurge — https://edsurge.com/news/the-missing-middle-why-kids-still-struggle-to-read-after-third-grade

### 4) Code review is splitting into risk tiers: blast-radius triage + review the plan/schema, not the implementation
- Track: better_decisions
- Marker: 🔥 do today
- Why it matters to me: PR volume up 5× in 3 years (growth accelerating since end-2025). Named operators (Weaviate, Duckbill, OpenAI, Anthropic, Uber) are converging on: AI does the review, humans make scope/risk decisions; low blast radius (not touching public API, auth, schema) → AI-only path; high → mandatory human. Some teams now review the plan/tests/database schema instead of the implementation. Duckbill (5-person team, 60-PR backlog): +94% PR throughput, 1h median merge on the no-human-review path, with guardrails (85% test floor, all linters on).
- Tradeoff / tension: Noise is the killer — WeTravel measured AI review and still declined adoption because signal wasn't high enough; Uber's uReview exists purely to grade/merge/drop AI comments. Ditching review costs tokens + attention, and UI/feel is not covered by tests.
- Implication: Reusable beyond code: any review-heavy workflow (content briefs, reports, campaign approvals) gets a blast-radius layer. And "review the schema/state, not the code" is a general attention/impact heuristic — the product's state machine is the hard, rigid part; everything else regenerates.
- Action: This week: define our 5 "high blast radius" categories (public API, auth, billing, data schema, agent skills) and route low-risk changes to AI-only merge.
- Confidence: high (multiple named operators with before/after numbers)
- Source: Pragmatic Engineer — https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/what-is-happening-with-code-reviews
